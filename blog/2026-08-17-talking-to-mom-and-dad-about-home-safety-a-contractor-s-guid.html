Talking to Mom and Dad About Home Safety: A Contractor's Guide to Difficult Conversations

It’s August, and for many of us here in Northeast Nebraska, that means enjoying the last bits of summer—maybe a county fair, working in the garden, or just sitting on the porch as the sun sets. But for some, August also brings a subtle, nagging worry: you've started noticing little changes in your aging parents. Maybe Mom stumbled coming up the steps, or Dad has trouble getting out of the bathtub after a long day in the heat. You know their home, the one they've lived in for decades, might not be as safe as it once was, but how do you even begin to talk to them about making modifications?

I’ve seen this scenario play out countless times in my years as a contractor here at Watts ATP Contractor. It's a conversation filled with love, concern, and often, a lot of unspoken fears—on both sides. Parents cherish their independence, and adult children worry about overstepping or making their parents feel "old." But waiting until a fall or an accident forces the issue often means more stress, more expense, and a much harder recovery. My goal today is to give you some practical, hands-on advice, not just about what modifications to make, but how to approach that crucial first conversation.
